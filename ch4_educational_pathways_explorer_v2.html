What is an educational pathway?

An educational pathway is the route you take to learn new skills and gain qualifications after school. There is no single right path — different pathways suit different people and different careers.

Formal pathways
Lead to a recognised qualification — like a certificate, diploma, or degree. Employers and universities accept these.
Non-formal pathways
Build skills without a formal qualification — like short courses, volunteering, or work experience.

Six educational pathways

Here are six common pathways in Australia. Read through each one carefully — you will use these to answer Question 1 in your workbook.

🛠 Apprenticeship
Learn a trade while working and earning money. You study part-time at TAFE alongside your job. Common in construction, electrical, plumbing, and cooking.
💼 Traineeship
Similar to an apprenticeship — work and study at the same time. Usually in service industries like retail, hospitality, or business administration.
🏫 TAFE / VET course
Study at a TAFE or training college. Earn a Certificate or Diploma in a practical area like health, IT, creative arts, or construction.
🎓 University degree
Study at a university for 3+ years to earn a bachelor degree. Suits careers like teaching, nursing, engineering, or law.
✅ Short course / Micro-credential
A focused course in one skill — often done online in hours to weeks. Examples: first aid, food safety, forklift licence, barista training.
⭐ VCE / VCE VM
Complete your senior secondary certificate. VCE VM (what you are doing now!) is the applied learning pathway, recognised by the VCAA.

Question 3 — let’s look at an example first

Before you write your own paragraph, read through this example. It shows you exactly what a good answer looks like. Notice how it names two pathways and gives a pro and a con for each.

📖 Worked example
I am interested in working in the early childhood education industry. Two pathways I could take are a TAFE Certificate III in Early Childhood Education and a University degree in Education.

The TAFE pathway is practical and hands-on, which suits the way I like to learn. A pro is that I could finish in one to two years and start working sooner. A con is that some higher-level roles, like a director, may expect me to have a degree later on.

The university pathway would give me deeper knowledge and open more doors in the long run. A pro is that it leads to more career options over time. A con is that it takes three to four years and involves a lot more reading and written assessments.
👉 See how it: names the industry → names two pathways → gives a pro AND a con for each one.
